your memories, your emotions, your sky.

We kept noticing the same thing happening to us.

Something small would happen — a photo that made us smile, a random thought at 2am, a voice note we recorded just to get something off our chest — and within a day or two it would just... disappear into the noise. Buried under a hundred other photos, a hundred other notes.

None of us wanted another journaling app. Honestly, we'd all downloaded one before and deleted it within a week. Lists of entries with dates on them just don't feel like memories. They feel like homework.

So we started asking a dumb question: what if a memory wasn't an entry? What if it was a star?

That question turned into Celstra.

---

So, what it actually is

Celstra is a place to save your thoughts, photos, voice notes, whatever — and instead of stacking them in a feed, each one shows up as a star in your own sky.

If a few memories feel connected — like they're part of the same chapter of your life — you can link them into a constellation. So you're not just scrolling back through your past, you're kind of... looking up at it.

---

What you can do with it

Write something down. Whatever's actually on your mind, not what should be.

Add a photo or a voice note, for the moments words don't quite cover.

Tag the mood. Let the memory carry how you actually felt, not just what happened.

Connect memories that belong together and turn them into a constellation.

Come back later and search, filter, revisit — find the stars that still mean something.

---

Why stars, though?

Because memories were never really a list to begin with.

Some are loud and bright. Some barely register until much later. Some are hard to look at directly. And some only make sense once you see them next to each other.

A sky just fit better than a spreadsheet.

One memory, one star.
A few that belong together, a constellation.
All of it together, your sky.

---

How we actually built it

It's a web app — the frontend does the heavy lifting on the sky itself: rendering stars, constellations, moods, the whole interactive feel. The backend just quietly handles storing everything behind the scenes.

We added support for photos and voice recordings early on because we didn't want people limited to typing.

Mostly, we just tried to stay out of our own way. The tech is there to hold the feeling, not show off.

---

What's left

A lot, honestly. We want the sky to feel more personal over time, make old memories easier to stumble back onto, make constellations easier to build, and give people more ways to actually sit with their own memories instead of just storing them.

But the core of it isn't going to change:

a place to slow down and look back at what made you, you.

---

Last thing

Celstra isn't trying to replace your notebook, or your camera roll, or your voice memos app.

It's just trying to make you want to open them again.

Because sometimes all it takes is a few little stars to remind you how far you've actually come.

Celstra — your memories, your emotions, your sky.
